# Observer names

The standard build writes `output/observers/names.csv`, `evidence.csv` and `review.csv`. This supplementary inventory sits alongside the five scientific tables. Native attendance text is preserved in the survey table.

`raw/reports/observers.csv` is the saved historical publication extract: one supplied name per source, with local PDF path, one-based PDF page number, publication year, context and extraction method. It is editable and does not require the local PDFs, Poppler or OCR during a build. Publication years describe the source, not necessarily the years a person observed. Participants are retained; photographers, cited researchers, funders and groups are excluded unless also explicitly listed as observers.

The initial inventory covers participant lists in annual reports from 2008–2021 and 2023–2025 and observer acknowledgements in the 1996 first paper and 2019 paper. The short 2004–2007 reports contain no named participant lists; the 2022 full report is unavailable. The 2020 paper refers back to the 2019 acknowledgements. OCR-derived spellings remain in the review output; extraction is a working historical inventory, not a verified complete register of people. Original spellings, initials and nicknames may still denote the same person.

`config/observers/name_aliases.csv` manages duplicates with columns `alias,observer_name,note`. Every alias points directly to the final preferred name. Case, accents, punctuation, whitespace and the order of surname/initials are normalized for comparison. This merges spelling formatting only; there is no fuzzy matching or automatic expansion of an unknown initial. The initial aliases cover established recurring names found in both attendance logs and publications. Ambiguous names such as `M. Maire` remain unresolved.

New Trektellen names enter the list automatically when new paired exports are read. To review an unfamiliar spelling, inspect `review.csv` and its source context, then add an alias only when the identity is established. Use a self-mapping row to accept a preferred full name. A canonical target cannot point to another alias; conflicting normalized aliases stop the build.

`names.csv` has one row per resolved or supplied name, its original variants, source types, first/last source years, number of evidence rows and identity status. `supplied_name` means a name was supplied, not that the person's identity has been independently verified. `unresolved` marks initials or a single name; `mapped_alias` marks an explicit crosswalk. `evidence_rows` counts distinct source/name/context rows, not visits or observer effort. `evidence.csv` preserves the attribution behind every name. `review.csv` lists unparsed attendance text, incomplete identities, unmapped names and OCR spellings. Review rows do not block scientific count processing.

Historical extraction initially used only the workbook's `Pression observation` observer field; the additional historical review described below extends this coverage. Trektellen extraction uses header `observers`, not numeric headcounts or account names (`createdby`/`changedby`). Free-text bird remarks are not mined indiscriminately for names, because citations and named people do not necessarily identify observers. Attendance clocks are removed only from the inventory; supplied clocks remain in the original data. Unparsed fragments are retained for review.

Run `uv run python scripts/build_dataset.py` after editing either input. Outputs and their input hashes are recorded in `output/metadata.json` and published only when the existing build checks pass.

## Acknowledgements export

`acknowledgements.txt` contains a single paragraph of initials and surnames, sorted by surname. `acknowledgements.md` uses the same names in a single French thank-you paragraph; `acknowledgements.csv` retains full or supplied names, abbreviated labels, source types and spelling variants. Initials supplied without a recoverable full given name are included, for example `R. Nussbaumer`; unidentified first names and codes remain in the review files. These are drafts for acknowledgement preparation. Given-name/surname order is corrected where the source explicitly capitalizes the surname, all-capital words use regular casing, and supplied accents and mixed-case names are preserved. Exact duplicate formatted names are removed, and reviewed spelling aliases apply before export.

`incomplete_names.csv` keeps single names, codes and ambiguous fragments that cannot be formatted with a supplied surname. `possible_duplicates.csv` lists similar full names for review; similarity is only a review aid and does not establish identity. Do not assume differently named people are duplicates solely because their names are similar. Complete these reviews before treating the acknowledgements as an exhaustive list without remaining identity duplicates.

The typo review consolidates explicit spelling variants and recurring abbreviations in `names.csv` itself, including reversed name order, rather than only formatting the acknowledgements export. Each original spelling remains in `evidence.csv` and `name_variants`. Corrections are saved as aliases; future names are never fuzzily merged without a saved decision. Similarity candidates were reviewed using annual participant lists, recurring attendance and paired attendees. Three full-name pairs remain unresolved: Antoine/Antonin Branchu, Gaël/Gaëlle Parchet, and Pascal Olivier/Pascal-Olivier Roy. Names are now formatted before checking for initials, so all-capital surnames such as `MAS` and `DI CAPUA` do not incorrectly exclude full names from acknowledgements.


## Historical coverage audit

The earlier extraction was not exhaustive. The supplementary extract `raw/reports/historical_observers.csv` now preserves names recovered from original attendance comments and explicit observer attributions in count remarks. This includes a missed attribution to Kelig Gourvennec in an archived observation remark; Hugo Touzé is mentioned there as a committee reviewer and is not treated as the observer. Counts rejected during identification review can still credit people who made the observation.

`raw/reports/observer_source_coverage.csv` records hashes, sheet/column coverage, row counts and non-empty cell counts for the four sheets in `count_2021.xlsx` and relevant columns in all four archived historical workbooks. Header inventories identify summary sheets without observer fields. Relevant columns include native `Commentaire de la liste`, remarks, details, private remarks and observer/transmitter fields wherever present. The archived 2020 export's observer and transmitter fields identify only the collective, so they cannot supply individual observers. Archived `Alldata.Nom` identifies the site, rather than a person. These workbooks are local evidence; builds read the saved extraction and audit tables, not the ignored archive files.

`historical_extraction_review.csv` records review decisions for 55 fragments that the original attendance parser did not understand, including accepted supplied names, weather/effort text, and two still-unresolved attendance fragments. Giles and David are supplied without surnames and remain incomplete. A field scan cannot establish names absent from the source, identify unnamed visitors, or recover the missing full 2022 report. The audit establishes what was checked; it does not certify a complete historical register of individuals.

Initials can coincide for different people. `abbreviation_collisions.csv` preserves all full or supplied names sharing an abbreviated label. The paragraph prints each normalized label once, while `names.csv` keeps their identities separate unless an explicit alias consolidates them.

`name_decisions.csv` explicitly excludes reviewed collective labels and incomplete personal fragments from `names.csv` and acknowledgements while retaining their source evidence. `Nos Oiseaux` and `CPN Poissy` are groups; `Jacques (BE)` supplies no surname. Malformed `Bisetti J,` and OCR `Schmidt Ÿ.` are preserved only as original variants of Jean Bisetti and Yvan Schmidt, respectively. Editorial acknowledgement roles and a manuscript draft are maintained separately in the local `paper/acknowledgements/` directory, with `paper/scripts/make_acknowledgements.py` as the authoring command.
